# Zweiter Audit, 15. November 2026

Der erste Review (`REVIEW-2026-11.md`) wurde durch Lesen gemacht und sagte das
auch: DAST fand nicht statt. Dieser hier ist der Gegenentwurf — jeder Befund
musste mit einem Test reproduziert werden, der aus dem genannten Grund
scheitert, und nur was dabei wirklich rot wurde, steht hier.

## Verfahren

Neun unabhängige Linsen, jede mit konkreten Dateien und Suchmustern statt
„schau mal drüber":

| Linse | Was sie sucht |

50 Befunde. Jeder kam vor einen Skeptiker mit dem Auftrag, ihn zu widerlegen —
ausdrücklich nachsichtig, weil einen echten Fehler zu töten schlimmer ist, als
einen zweifelhaften in die nächste Stufe zu lassen. 11 fielen dabei.

Die 39 Überlebenden gingen in die Reproduktionsstufe: ein Test schreiben, ihn
scheitern sehen, die Ausgabe als Beleg mitliefern, den Test wieder löschen.
Sequenziell im warmen Arbeitsverzeichnis, weil gleichzeitige `swift build`
sich um dieselbe Sperre streiten und ein nicht kompilierender Entwurf jedem
anderen Agenten den Build zerschießt.

**38 reproduziert** — 13 schwer, 20 mittel, 5 leicht. Keiner zerfiel in der
Reproduktion, was heißt, dass die Skeptiker-Stufe sauber sortiert hat. Der
39. wurde nicht mehr gemessen; der Lauf wurde nach 6,5 Stunden abgebrochen.

Allen Linsen wurden die bereits behobenen und die bewusst offenen Punkte des
ersten Reviews mitgegeben, damit niemand sie ein zweites Mal „findet".

## Was dabei an mir selbst gefunden wurde

Zwei Fehler in diesen Korrekturen fand die CI, nicht ich:

* Der SIGTERM-Handler hielt seine Signalquelle in einer lokalen Variable. Im
  Debug-Build hält sie, im Release optimiert der Compiler sie weg — damit
  blieb `SIG_IGN` stehen und `mediafin --watch` war per SIGTERM nicht mehr zu
  beenden. Der Smoke-Test meldete das nicht, sondern **hing 25 Minuten**, weil
  sein `wait` auf einen Prozess wartete, der nie endet. Beides behoben; das
  Skript schickt jetzt nach der Gnadenfrist SIGKILL und scheitert.
* Das Aufräumen eines halb gescheiterten Umzugs hätte beim *Austausch* die
  zurückgeholte Bibliothekskopie gelöscht. Die Tests aus Befund 7 sagten es
  beim ersten Lauf: „the library lost its own copy".

Und zwei Messlatten flatterten auf dem macOS-Läufer, bevor klar war, warum:

| | 1 500 Dateien schreiben | 500 Dateien lesen | Verhältnis |
|---|---|---|---|
| dieser Container | 1,2 s | 0,4 s | 0,3 |
| macOS-Läufer | 0,38 s | 4,3 s | 11 |

Eine Größe, die sich zwischen zwei Maschinen um das Dreißigfache
unterscheidet, misst nicht den Code. Die Regel steht jetzt als **Zahl** da:
der Test zählt die Verzeichnisauflistungen. **1** bei 200 Videos, **200** ohne
den Zwischenspeicher — maschinenunabhängig, und in 0,4 s rot statt nach 40 s.

`tools/check-unused.py` macht den Build rot, wenn eine Funktion oder

## Nachtrag: what the audit could not see

All 38 reproduced findings were about code that runs. None of them was about
code that *cannot* run, and the larger fault was exactly that: of the
eighteen commands the engine accepts, five had no sender anywhere in the
program.

- `findConvertible`, `convertFiles`, `cancelConversions` — the whole
  conversion feature. FFmpeg driving, progress reporting, cancelling, size
  estimation and their tests were all in the binary, the settings window
  offered no conversion options at all, and the traffic light cheerfully
  reported "Gefunden — Konvertieren ist möglich". It had been reachable from
  the web front-end and from nothing else since that front-end was removed.
- `testJellyfinConnection` — and the Jellyfin address had no field either:
  the setting was in `WebSettings`, `SettingsMerge` wrote it back, the engine
  read it, and nothing could type it in.
- `clearHistory`.

Five of the sixteen names in `tools/unused-baseline.txt` were that missing
interface's display helpers. The baseline was the only thing in the project
that knew, and it recorded them as debt rather than as a symptom.

### What this says about the method

The audit read the code and asked whether each path was right. That cannot
find a path with no entrance. Three checks were added for the class:

1. `tools/check-unused.py` now fails the build on a new declaration used only
   by tests, and its baseline is empty. A leftover has to be deleted or
   given a caller on the day it appears.
2. The smoke modes press the buttons: `--press-convert` runs a real FFmpeg
   conversion end to end, `--press-clear-history` answers the confirmation
   both ways, `--press-check` looks at the window while the engine holds its
   queue.
3. `--screenshot --panel NAME` renders every panel, because most of the
   window now lives behind the menu and the only picture anything ever took
   was of the main surface. That caught the conversion list drawing nothing
   at all within a minute of existing.

The deepest one found this way was not a missing button. Four commands hold
the engine's serial queue without ever setting `busy`, and the window gated
everything on `busy`: for up to a minute it said "Bereit" with Rückgängig
live, and a second press there reverses a batch nobody asked to reverse,
because the first press has already dropped the transaction it worked from.
`StatusSnapshot.isWorking` had been in the file the whole time, with a
comment naming the failure in the past tense, and no reader.

## Second Nachtrag: the fixes themselves, read by six adversaries

The work above added about a thousand lines in one day — buttons for the
five dead commands, the gate, the estimate, three new checks — and then a
separate pass read only those lines, in six parts, with every finding put
through two independent attempts to refute it. Twenty-two survived. Every
one was checked against the code again before anything was changed, and
every fix got a Gegenprobe that was watched failing first.

That is the uncomfortable number: the change that fixed a class of fault
contained twenty more of its own, and nine of them were the *same* class —
a report that cannot be wrong, a check that cannot fail.

The ones worth remembering:

- **The gate waited half a second.** `undoLast()` submitted and returned
  without touching the widget, and the only thing that applied the new
  `isWorking` gate was the 500 ms refresh timer. So the button the gate
  exists to shut stayed pressable for up to half a second after the press —
  which is exactly where the second click of a double-click lands. The fix
  the previous section describes was, for a real user, not yet a fix. The
  check could not see it either, because it called `refresh()` itself
  before reading the sensitivity.
- **Leeren was never gated at all**, and the undo count its question names
  is read at press time while `.clearHistory` runs later on the serial
  queue. With an empty journal the dialog said "Im Moment gibt es nichts
  rückgängig zu machen", the rename in flight then recorded its
  transaction, and the queued clear destroyed it. The dialog promised there
  was nothing to lose.
- **Apply reported success for saves that never happened.** `save()`
  refuses the whole write on any `problems()`, and the window never learned:
  it printed "Übernommen um HH:MM:SS" with a clock that moved on every
  press while `settings.json` stayed byte-identical. The refusal's own text
  goes to `lastResult`, drawn on the main window — behind the settings
  panel the user is looking at. It also advanced its merge baseline to the
  values it had just failed to write, and the merge writes back only fields
  whose widget differs from that baseline: one mistyped quality value
  silently dropped the codec change made in the same breath.
- **`check-reachable.py` could not report four of the eighteen.** Its
  needle was the bare substring `.commandName`, and the window names four
  of its own methods after the commands they send — `{ $0.clearHistory() }`
  matches `.clearHistory` on its own. Deleting the `submit` inside
  `clearHistory()` left it printing "ok : all 18 engine commands have a
  sender". Two of the four blind spots were among the five dead commands
  whose discovery is the entire reason the file exists. Its docstring
  argued this could not happen.
- **The Apply step never pressed Apply.** `pressApply()` called
  `saveFolders()` directly, so a ~120-line CI step tested that method while
  the button itself could have been unwired or packed into a container
  nobody shows. Every press helper now goes through `gtk_button_clicked`,
  and the one button that had just been moved into a new footer is also
  checked for being mapped.
- **"pressed stop while idle: yes"** was printed unconditionally and
  grepped for by CI — a tautology dressed as an assertion, which passed
  with the Stopp button's whole wiring deleted.
- **The Undo gate was asserted only in the "false" direction**, so "Undo is
  gated correctly" was indistinguishable from "Undo is never pressable at
  all". The line that would have shown the difference was already being
  printed and nothing read it.
- **Three different states drew one sentence.** Never searched, searched and
  found nothing, and converted everything all read "Noch nichts gesucht —
  „Suchen" drücken." A finished run left a panel denying it had happened.
- **Stopp was reported as a failure of the file.** Terminating the encoder
  makes it exit non-zero, so the user's own Stopp landed in the same
  `catch` a broken file does: red row with an FFmpeg exit code, "Mit
  Fehlern beendet" in the header, and the file dropped off the candidate
  list although nothing had been done to it.
- **The resolution estimate was clipped at 1.0**, so for a 1080p source
  "2160p (4K)" and "keep the source resolution" came out identical — the
  window collapsed them to one number and reported a 38 % saving for a run
  that writes `-vf scale=-2:2160` and produces a bigger file. The test
  meant to cover it was called `testATargetAboveTheSourceChangesNothing`
  and asserted the bug, directly against its own comment.
- **`--press-clear-history` shipped in the release binary**, was documented
  in `--help`, and ran against the real configuration directory: it scans,
  renames everything the scan proposes, then clears the undo journal while
  answering its own confirmation "yes" from inside the process.

And one found while writing the test for another: pressing Stopp between
two files, or in the moment before FFmpeg comes up, armed nothing and
answered "Es konvertiert gerade nichts." while the bar advanced —
`cancelConversions` looked only at a running process and a queued run, and
both read empty in those gaps.

## Third Nachtrag: the fixes read again, by six more adversaries

The same method, over the commits of the second pass (`31668b3..143e5b9`):
six reviewers with one dimension each, every finding put through a code lens
and a scenario lens that were told to break the code and watch the new
checks stay green. Sixteen came back confirmed, seven refuted. I checked all
sixteen against the source myself before touching anything; all sixteen were
real.

### What the program was saying that was not so

- **A stopped conversion left its dead encoder behind.** The stop path
  breaks out of the `for` from inside the `catch`, jumping over the one line
  that cleared `_runningConversion`, and the run's tail did not clear it
  either. `cancelConversions` reads that pointer to decide whether there is
  anything to stop, so afterwards every press while idle armed the cancel
  flag against a terminated process, answered "Stoppt nach der aktuellen
  Datei." with nothing running, and killed the next conversion on its first
  file — before FFmpeg had started. Self-perpetuating: that run breaks
  before `onStart`, so nothing overwrote the pointer. The gate's own comment
  was written to prevent exactly this; it came back through a different
  variable.
- **Übernehmen reported a save it had only queued.** A write the engine
  accepted and then could not perform left "Übernommen um HH:MM:SS" on
  screen, with the reason on the window behind the panel — and the merge
  baseline had advanced to values that were never stored, so the next press
  compared every edit equal to that baseline and dropped it. The edit was
  lost for good. Watched doing it: quality 26 typed, write failed, clock
  printed, and pressing Apply again with the file writable left the engine
  on 24.
- **The music folder was read by Übernehmen and thrown away nine lines
  later** by a merge that starts again from `stored`. It was the only
  `problems()`-validated field the window could not write, which made an
  invalid stored music folder a dead end: every Apply refused, and no
  control could correct it.
- **The conversion panel announced "Fertig. Die neuen Dateien liegen neben
  den Originalen" after runs in which every file had failed.** It inferred
  "converted" from "the list went empty", and the engine emptied the list
  for failures as readily as for successes — so the files were gone from the
  list too, with nothing to retry.
- **And it said "Keine Dateien zum Konvertieren gefunden." when the engine
  had refused to look at all.** No library folder configured: it returns at
  its first guard without setting `busy`, and the flip out of "Wird gesucht
  …" keyed on the engine being idle. Any unrelated job pinned the panel on
  "Wird gesucht …" for the same reason. A fresh install hits this on the
  first press of Suchen.
- **The one Stopp outside the menu had nothing to act on during a search.**
  `findConvertible` sets `busy` and nothing else; the button is sensitive on
  bare `busy` and `cancelScan` covers scans and renames only. It is verbatim
  the fault `stopScan`'s dispatcher says it fixed, for the one busy-setter
  it has no branch for.

### Checks that could not fail, again

- **The guard on the three file-moving modes tested nothing.** Its whole
  predicate was "MEDIAFIN_CONFIG_DIR is non-empty" — and that variable is
  the documented, first-precedence way to relocate a *real* configuration.
  So the supported way to point `--press-clear-history` at a real library
  was the thing that satisfied the guard meant to stop it. Nothing pressed
  the guard either: all twenty invocations in the workflow set the variable
  or pass `--demo`.
- **`applyButtonIsOnScreen` was `gtk_widget_get_mapped`**, which a
  GtkScrolledWindow's viewport sets for its whole child whatever the scroll
  offset. Measured both ways with Übernehmen packed back into the scroller:
  mapped `true`, the tightened predicate `false`, and the press still
  reported a save — which is why clicking cannot catch this.
- **Both conversion-panel empty-state assertions were a blacklist of the one
  sentence the four-state split replaced**, so three of the four states were
  interchangeable. Removing the `.converted` assignment and running the real
  mode printed "Keine Dateien zum Konvertieren gefunden." after a successful
  run, and the old assertion passed.
- **The "no percentage for a silent job" check ran where `busy` is false** —
  the one state in which the code before and after that fix behave
  identically. The rule now lives in Core as `HeaderSubtitle`, where the
  state it needs is three lines; six of its assertions fail on the old rule.
- **The second-run block's comment claimed to watch the `pending.isEmpty`
  branch.** That branch needs ids no longer on the list, and the mode
  presses Konvertieren once. It has a unit test now.
- **`--press-convert` read the three "while converting" values from a
  refresh taken before the snapshot that decided to print them**, and CI
  read exactly that one at-risk sample with `head -1`.
- **`check-unused.py` could not report a name declared in two shapes.** The
  bare-name count put a function's call sites and a property's own body in
  one total, so the dead no-argument `estimatedSizePercent` passed with five
  mentions against two declarations. Counting the shapes apart found a
  second one nothing had ever called: `FFprobeMetadataProvider.defaultPath`.
  Both are gone.

### Found while fixing, not acted on

`EmbeddedMetadataProvider` and `FFprobeMetadataProvider` are never
constructed anywhere in the program. Only their static parsing helpers are
exercised, by tests. Whether embedded-metadata identification is meant to be
wired up is a design question, not a fix, so it is written down here rather
than decided in passing.

Unchanged from the second Nachtrag: C4 (prompt injection via filename into

### Two of the refuted seven, looked at again

Two findings the panel threw out were right about the mechanism and wrong
only about the label, and both are worth the fix.

**The API key was stored above the refusal.** The key and the server address
live in two stores and are paired only when a request is made, so a refused
Apply left the NEW key with the OLD address — and the field was cleared, so
nothing on screen said where the key had gone. Someone moving to a different
model server who mistypes a quality value in the same press then has
"Verbindung testen" send their new credential, as a Bearer token, to the
server they were leaving. The code's reason for the old order was "losing a
key the user just typed because a quality field has a typo would be its own
fault" — true, and better answered by leaving the key in the field, which is
what it does now. Measured both ways: before, `secrets after the refusal:
["local-llm-api-key"]` with the address still the old one; after, `[]` with
the key still in the field, and both landing together on the corrected press.

**The two "straight after the press" gate assertions press Alles prüfen, not
the buttons they name.** The refuter's own Gegenprobe is the proof: with
`undoLast()` put back to a bare `submit`, the mode still printed `false` for
both gates and the CI block passed. The assertion does catch `start()`

### One of the refuted seven that is real, and open

The size estimate collapses two different answers into one number at high
quality. `ConversionEstimator` clamps the product with `min(1.5, …)`, and at
a low quality value both probes the window takes -- "keep the source
resolution" and "2160p" -- saturate at that ceiling. The window then takes
its `normal == fourK` branch, whose whole meaning is "this choice is
source-independent", and prints one number: "Schätzung: etwa 150 % der
ursprünglichen Größe." For h264 at quality 17 that is the same sentence for
two settings that are not the same, and the source-independence it implies
is not true there.

The review panel refuted this as a finding for the range it was reviewing,
and that was right: the behaviour is byte-identical before and after those
commits -- a reviewer restored the pre-diff guard and measured both. It is
not a regression. It is a pre-existing wrong report, and it is written down
here because I said I would write it down and then did not, which is how a
known fault becomes an unknown one.

What a fix looks like: either say the number is a floor when the estimate
is clamped ("mindestens 150 %"), or stop treating two saturated values as
evidence of source-independence. Not done, not scheduled.
